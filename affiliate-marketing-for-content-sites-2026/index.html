Affiliate Marketing on a Content Site: What Actually Pays in 2026

Affiliate income and ad income respond to opposite strategies. Running them together badly is the most common mistake.

Display ads and affiliate links are different businesses with the same traffic. Optimising

for one while running the other is why most sites plateau. This is what each one actually

rewards, and how to run both.

The fundamental difference

Display adsAffiliate
Pays forAttention, any pageIntent, specific page
Best pagesHigh-traffic informationalHigh-intent comparison and "how to"
RPMFixed by niche and geo5–50× higher on the right page
Scales withPageviewsConversions
RiskAd blockers, policy strikesProgram changes, commission cuts
Time lagImmediate30–90 days from click to payout

A page with 400 views and 6 signups earns more from affiliate than 4,000 views of a

definitional article earn from ads. The traffic that earns the most is traffic that will

never pass display-ad approval in the first place.

Two structural facts worth internalising. First, **cookie windows matter more than

commission size** — a 20% program with a 180-day window beats 40% with 24 hours. Second,

recurring commissions compound, so one SaaS program with monthly residuals is worth

more than four one-off payouts.

The structure that works

1. Affiliate links only where intent exists

Not on every page. Reserve them for pages where the reader has a decision to make:

  • "Best X for Y" comparisons
  • "X vs Y" comparisons
  • "Is X worth it for a team my size"
  • Pricing and migration pages
  • "X alternatives"

A pricing page that answers real questions is a better affiliate asset than a 5,000-word

tutorial that links nothing.

2. Disclose above the fold on any page with affiliate links

Not buried in the footer. This is both an FTC requirement and a trust requirement, and it

costs you nothing measurable.

3. Link with intent, not with count

One strong contextual link beats eight banner links. Nine links on a page splits the

click and reads as spam. Three, placed where the reader is deciding, outperform thirty.

4. Track by page, not by total

The metric that matters is earnings per 1,000 pageviews, by page. That number tells you

which articles to write more of, and it is the only honest way to know whether affiliate

links are earning or just decorating. Most sites have no idea this number varies 30× between

their pages.

5. Build the email list from the intent pages

A visitor who requested a comparison, then left a comparison page, is your most valuable

person. Their conversion rate on email is several times a general reader's, and the affiliate

click is the only revenue the site needs to keep running in month two.

For a monetized content site in a high-value niche with English-language traffic:

150,000$22–35$3,300–5,25060–80%

The crossover point the industry keeps citing — around 50,000 monthly pageviews — is real.

Below it, ads carry you and affiliates are a bonus. Above it, affiliate programs and direct

sponsorships usually out-earn the ad network, and the ad network becomes the smaller part

of the business.

The mistakes that cap affiliate income

  1. Links without comparison content. A link in the middle of a listicle converts a fraction of a link inside a genuine comparison.
  2. Programs chosen for commission rate instead of fit. A 30% program with a product nobody buys is worth less than 10% on something your readers actually need.
  3. Not disclosing, which is both a legal requirement and the fastest way to lose reader trust permanently.
  4. No page-level tracking, so you cannot tell which topics make money. Everything else is guesswork.
  5. Treating it as a link farm. The sites that got devalued en masse in recent years all look like that. Original verification is now the moat.
  6. Depending on one program. Programs change rates and terminate relationships. Have at least three, and no one above 50% of affiliate revenue.

What to actually do first

Pick the three programs that match your niche and its buying intent. Write one genuinely

excellent comparison page per program, with real testing. Track revenue per page for 60

days. Then write more pages in the direction that earned, not the direction that is fun.

That is the whole strategy, and it is why sites that follow it consolidate while sites that

add "10 more affiliate links" do not.
