Affiliate Disclosure

Full affiliate and sponsorship disclosure. This page exists because the FTC, the ASA and Google's own program policies require it, and because you should be able to see how we make money before you decide whether to trust us.

How StackSignal makes money

  1. Affiliate commissions. Some links to vendors are affiliate links, marked with ⟶ in our articles. If you click one and buy, we may get a commission at no extra cost to you. Commission rates are set by the vendor and are not negotiable by us.
  2. Display advertising. Advertising is served through Google AdSense and, as we grow, other networks. Advertisers do not choose which reviews run, and we do not accept advertising that targets a page's editorial conclusion.
  3. Sponsorships. Occasionally a sponsor funds a piece we had already decided to publish. Sponsors get a link and nothing else — no review, no score, no placement in a ranking. Sponsored pieces are labelled in the byline area.

What money cannot buy here

  • Placement in a "best X" list.
  • A rating or score.
  • Removal or softening of criticism.
  • A page being taken down or deindexed.
  • Being called the best tool in a category.

What we will always tell you

If we use a tool and it did not work for us, the review says so, including about tools whose affiliate programs pay us. If we have not tested something, we do not score it — we either label it untested or leave it out.

Editorial review standard

Every scorecard on StackSignal is published on the methodology page before the review runs. Criteria are not adjusted mid-review to produce a convenient answer.
