AdSense Approval in 2026: The Checklist That Gets You Approved

Most rejections are for the same six reasons, and all of them are fixable before you apply.

AdSense rejects most sites that apply too early, and the rejection email rarely tells you

which specific page failed. This is the checklist we work from, and the reasons that

actually appear in rejections.

The requirements, plainly

Before you apply, all of these must be true:

  • Original content, published on your own domain. Not rewritten, not translated by a

machine, not aggregated.

  • Privacy Policy, About, Contact, and Terms pages, each substantial enough to be a real

page. Not one-paragraph stubs.

  • A working contact method. A form that goes somewhere, or a real email. A form that

posts to nowhere is a common silent failure.

  • Enough content to look like a real publication. In practice this means 25+ substantial

articles. There is no published number; 25 is where approval odds change sharply.

  • No prohibited content. No adult, no gambling or betting, no how-to-get-rich-quick,

no unlicensed copyrighted material, no "hacked" software or cheats.

  • A clean domain history. No prior AdSense policy strikes on the domain.

The six reasons sites actually get rejected

1. Low-value content

By far the most common. Google defines it as content that does not add value beyond what a

searcher could get elsewhere — paraphrases of existing articles, common-sense definitions,

listicles with no testing behind them.

The distinguishing question: *could this page have been written by someone who had never used

the product being described?* If yes, it is low-value, and no amount of polish fixes it.

The fix is expensive and simple. Every page needs at least one original, verifiable thing —

a test result, a measured comparison, a number from your own use, a decision explained with

a reason. Ten such pages beats fifty paraphrased ones.

2. Not enough content overall

Sites with five posts get rejected for "insufficient content" regardless of quality. Under

25 substantial posts, apply later. This is the single most common self-inflicted rejection.

3. Missing policy pages

Google now checks for them directly. Requirements beyond the page existing:

  • Privacy Policy — must name the data you collect, cookies, third-party vendors including

ad networks, and how to opt out. A generic template that does not mention your ad partner

is the usual failure.

  • Contact — must include a working method and a real response commitment.
  • About — who is behind the site, editorial focus, and how content is produced.
  • Terms — use of the site, liability, affiliate disclosure if any.

4. Navigation and UX problems

Broken links, a missing menu, pages that do not load, a mobile layout that is unreadable, or

a site that appears to be under construction. Test on a real phone before submitting.

5. Copied or syndicated material

Reruns of another site's content, content lifted wholesale, or machine translation of

another site. If the post existed elsewhere first, the risk is not worth it.

6. Domain or previous account history

A domain previously banned from AdSense gets rejected regardless of current content.

There is no appeal on this one — the domain is the problem.

The pre-submission checklist

Run this literally before clicking submit.

  • [ ] 25+ original articles, each 800+ words of substance
  • [ ] At least one original verifiable data point in most posts
  • [ ] Privacy Policy names ad networks, cookies, and opt-out
  • [ ] Contact page with a working form or email
  • [ ] About page naming the person and describing the editorial process
  • [ ] Terms of Service page
  • [ ] Affiliate disclosure page, if you have any affiliate links
  • [ ] Site works on a real phone
  • [ ] No internal broken links
  • [ ] Custom domain on HTTPS
  • [ ] No content that requires cookies or login to view
  • [ ] No copied content anywhere
  • [ ] Author page or author byline with a real person named

If you get rejected

The email usually names a category but not a page. The response is the same:

  1. Do not reapply immediately. Reapplying within days of a rejection is close to

useless and can extend the block.

  1. Wait 2–3 weeks minimum while you fix the named issue plus the four most likely others.
  2. Add 10+ more substantial articles. This is what actually moves the outcome — a

resubmission with no new content gets the same answer.

  1. Rewrite your Privacy Policy to explicitly name Google AdSense, cookies, and the

opt-out link. This alone fixes a surprising share of rejections.

  1. Then resubmit once.

The realistic timeline

First application: 1–4 weeks for review. After a rejection and a real content push: 4–10

weeks. Sites that get approved on the first attempt are those that waited until the

checklist was genuinely complete, not the sites that applied soonest.

One thing to decide before you apply

AdSense is the floor, not the finish line. At monetized scale you will move to a network

with better RPM — but the approval is still the gate, and a site with 25 original tested

articles is also a site that affiliates and sponsors will trust. That is the real return on

doing this properly rather than padding.
