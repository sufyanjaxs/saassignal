About & Methodology

StackSignal is an independent English-language review site for B2B software, AI tooling, and the automation that connects them. It is written and operated by Sufyan Khan.

Why the site exists

Most "best software" content is written by someone who has never opened the product. It repeats the vendor's positioning, scores things on features that only matter to a 20-person company, and quietly ranks whatever pays the best commission. Readers cannot tell the difference, so the whole category gets less traffic and less trust.

What we do instead

We buy the tool on a real card, set it up against real work, and run it long enough to hit the boring failure modes: the migration, the permission model, the export, the billing change, the week after the champion leaves. Then we write what happened. Numbers come from dashboards, exports and invoices — not from a press release.

The scoring criteria

These five dimensions are fixed across every review. They are published here so a vendor cannot complain about a score after the fact, and so you can weight them yourself.

  • Time to first value (25%) — how long from signup to a real result you would show a client or a manager. Setup friction, onboarding quality, time-to-first-scheduled-task.
  • Reliability (25%) — uptime, rate limits, failed jobs, and what happens to your work when something breaks in the middle of the night.
  • Total cost at your scale (20%) — the real invoice, not the entry price: seats, usage overages, implementation hours, the tools you had to buy alongside it.
  • Depth where it matters (15%) — does it hold up at 50 users and 50,000 rows, or does it degrade into a spreadsheet with a UI?
  • Support (15%) — time to a human who can actually fix it, documentation quality, and whether they read the logs you sent.

Scores are shown as qualitative bands — Strong, Good, Mixed, Weak — so we are not claiming a precision the measurement does not support. If a tool does something in a way we cannot measure, we say that in the text instead of inventing a number.

What we refuse to do

  • Publish a score for something we did not use.
  • Rank by commission rate.
  • Accept payment to change a conclusion. See the advertise page and the disclosure for the exact terms.
  • Publish "10 best" lists of tools nobody has opened.
  • Run courses, autoresponder sequences impersonating a person, or fake urgency.

Corrections

Vendors and readers both catch things. If we have a fact wrong or a price stale, email with the detail and we will correct it and note the change on the article.

Disclosure

Advertising and affiliate links pay for the site. Neither influences a rating. How we make money is fully documented on the disclosure page.
