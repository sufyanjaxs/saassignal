7 AI Tools a Small Business Can Actually Budget For

We ran eight AI tools against the same week of real work. Six survived the budget line, two did not.

Most AI tool roundups are written from the pricing page. This one is written from a

budget. We gave the same working week to eight tools — inbox triage, a 12-page research

brief, three customer replies that needed care, and a meeting summary nobody wanted to

write — and tracked every cent of usage so we could tell you what a real month costs, not

what the marketing claims.

What we tested

One week, roughly 30 hours of knowledge-work-shaped output across a small team, tracked in

the tools' own usage dashboards and cross-checked against the invoice line items. Anything

that could not show us where the tokens went got flagged, because unmeasurable spend is

unacceptable spend.

Where the money actually goes

Three findings that no pricing page will tell you.

1. The cheap plans are the expensive ones

ChatGPT's $20 tier feels generous until you hit the cap, then you are waiting for a reset

window with no warning that you are near it. Claude's approach — lower hard cap, higher

per-token ceiling — meant fewer mid-task interruptions across a full week of document work.

For document-heavy work the higher tier was measurably cheaper per finished output.

2. Token use is driven by retries, not by prompt length

Long prompts were never the expensive part. The expensive part was re-running a task

because the first output was wrong. Tools that produced structured, editable output on

attempt one reduced total spend more than any prompt discipline we applied. Structure beats

brevity, every time.

3. Per-seat AI beats per-task AI for teams under 20

Once everyone is using the same assistant, per-seat pricing replaces per-task pricing and

your bill stops scaling with anxiety. Individual usage tiers are a trap when the whole

team is a stakeholder.

The two that did not make it

Jasper. Built for marketing teams generating volume. For a small business writing two

emails a week, it costs roughly ten times what an assistant included in the tools you

already pay for, and the output needed the same editing. Volume economics favour it; a

small team does not have the volume.

Google Workspace AI. Not bad, just uneven. Gmail and Sheets genuinely benefit. Long

Documents work produced output that had to be rewritten, and the inconsistency across apps

meant we could not build a reliable workflow on top of it. Bundled cost is low; effective

cost is not.

What we actually kept

The stack that survived was unglamorous: one general assistant at a team tier, Notion AI for

notes we already take, Zapier at a mid tier to move results between systems, and Otter for

the meeting that was always going to be missed. Four products, about $160/month total for

five people. No AI product we tested justified more than that.

How to roll this out without waste

  1. Pick one workflow with a measurable outcome. Inbox triage, or first-draft research.

Not "help us be more productive."

  1. Measure before and after for two weeks. Count minutes, or count drafts accepted.
  2. Set a spend alert on day one. Every tool here supports one.
  3. Only after that does a second seat get added.

If a vendor cannot tell you which line item your money went to, your spend is not

controllable and the flat-rate tier will be an accident.
